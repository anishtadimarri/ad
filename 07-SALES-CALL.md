# The Review Call — 30 Minutes

You are talking to someone who has already paid you $297 and has already heard their own front
desk lose a $40,000 patient. **Do not sell. Diagnose, then present.**

Close target: **25% of held calls.** Show rate: ~85% (they paid; paid buyers show).

---

## Structure

| Min | Phase | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Frame | Set the agenda, get permission |
| 2–6 | **The tape** | Play the worst 60 seconds. Then say nothing |
| 6–14 | Diagnose | Their four leaks, in their numbers |
| 14–22 | Present | The three modules, outcome-first |
| 22–27 | Price + guarantee | Then be quiet |
| 27–30 | Close or downsell | One objection handled, then D1 |

---

## 0–2 — Frame

> "Dr. [Name], thanks for the time. Here's how I'd like to use the next twenty minutes: I'll play
> you one of the three calls, we'll go through your scorecard, and I'll show you the three places
> your money is actually going. At the end, if it seems like something we should do together I'll
> tell you what that looks like. If not, you keep the report and the Blueprint and we're square.
> Sound fair?"

Get an explicit yes. It's the only permission you need for everything that follows.

---

## 2–6 — The tape

Play **60 seconds** of the Thursday 4:45pm call. Not the whole call — the worst minute.

**Then stop talking.**

Silence is the most important tool on this call. Let it run 5–10 seconds. He will speak first, and
what he says tells you exactly which objection you're handling later.

**Then, one question:**

> "How many times a week do you think that happens?"

Not "what did you think?" — that invites defensiveness about staff. "How many times a week" makes
it a volume problem, which is a systems problem, which is what you sell.

**If he defends the staff member:** agree immediately and completely.
> "Honestly, she wasn't bad — she was polite and she took a message. She just didn't have a system
> telling her what to do with a $40,000 caller. That's not a her problem."

**Never criticize their team.** They hired them. You'll lose the sale defending yourself instead of
selling.

---

## 6–14 — Diagnose

Walk the four leaks using **their** numbers from the calculator and the audit. Say the monthly
figure, not the annual one.

> "You said 25 consults a month, 28% same-day acceptance, $35,000 average case. Let me show you
> where that goes.
>
> **One — the phone.** Two of our three calls didn't result in a booked appointment. Scale that
> and you're losing more at the front desk than anywhere else in the practice.
>
> **Two — no-shows.** You said around 30%. That's 8 people a month you already paid roughly $300
> each to book, who never walked in. And behind each one, a $35,000 case.
>
> **Three — financing.** You're running one lender. One lender approves about half of full-arch
> applicants. Five, routed properly, gets to 75–80%. Every decline you don't re-route is a patient
> who wanted the treatment and walked out.
>
> **Four — the 'let me think about it' pile.** About 11 people a month. A full-arch decision takes
> three to seven months. Most practices follow up twice. So there are — conservatively — thirty or
> forty people sitting in your system right now who wanted this and never heard from you again."

**Then the total, monthly:**
> "Add three, four, and the no-shows — leave the phone out entirely — and it's about $179,000 a
> month of recoverable revenue. Roughly $107,000 of that is gross profit at your margins."

**Then stop and ask:**
> "Does that match what you'd have guessed, or is it higher?"

It's always higher. Now he's told you the problem is real, in his own words.

---

## 14–22 — Present

**Lead with outcomes. Mention mechanism only when asked.**

> "Three things, and I'll be specific about what we do and what we don't.
>
> **First, we get more of your booked consults to actually show up.** Instant response on every
> lead, a four-touch confirmation sequence, and a short video of you explaining what happens and
> roughly what it costs — so nobody arrives surprised. That takes no-shows from 30% to about 15%.
> On your volume that's five more people in the chair every month.
>
> **Second, they arrive already approved.** We apply to five lenders in your name and build the
> routing so a decline at one automatically goes to the next instead of ending the conversation.
> The pre-approval link goes in the confirmation sequence, so most patients walk in already
> knowing they're approved for $38,000. That alone recovers about two cases a month.
>
> **Third, we work the 'think about it' pile for six months.** Automated sequences plus specific
> call tasks that land in your system with a 48-hour deadline. Including the last three to six
> months of consults that never got followed up — that's usually where the first case comes from.
>
> **Two things we don't do.** We don't touch your marketing — your agency keeps their job and
> honestly they'll look better. And we don't coach your treatment coordinator. What happens in
> that room is yours. We handle everything on either side of it."

**Then:**
> "Any of that you'd want me to go deeper on?"

---

## 22–27 — Price and guarantee

Say the whole thing in one breath. No pause in the middle of a number.

> "It's $9,500 to install, half now and half when we go live on day 21. Then $2,500 a month, plus
> $300 for every consult that sits above your current baseline — so I get paid more when it's
> working and less when it isn't.
>
> And the guarantee: **five more consults sat per month within 90 days, or we work free until we
> get there.** Two conditions, and I'll be upfront about both because they protect you as much as
> me. You keep delivering at least 15 consults booked a month — if your lead flow drops for
> reasons that aren't mine, the clock pauses. And your consult person works the call tasks within
> 48 hours. I report that completion rate to you weekly so you can see whether the problem is us
> or the follow-through."

**Then be quiet.** Do not fill the silence. Do not add a bonus. Do not explain the price.

---

## 27–30 — Close

> "Want me to send the agreement?"

**If yes:** send within 5 minutes while you're still on the call. Get the first payment same day.
Book the kickoff before you hang up. Then offer U1 prepay.

**If hesitation:** handle exactly one objection, then go to D1. Do not handle three objections —
you'll talk yourself out of the downsell and out of the relationship.

---

# Objection library

### "I need to think about it"

> "Of course. Can I ask what specifically — the money, the timing, or whether it'll actually
> work?"

Then handle the real one. Never handle the stated one.

### "How much of this can my team just do themselves?"

> "Genuinely, most of it — the Blueprint I sent you is the real thing, not a teaser, and if your
> office manager builds it out you'll get real results. The reason practices pay us is that it's
> about 60 hours of setup and then somebody has to own it every week. If you've got someone with
> 60 spare hours and a reason to care, do it yourself. I'd rather tell you that than sell you
> something you don't need."

Say this sincerely. It closes more than any pressure would, and the ones who go build it
themselves come back in four months.

### "I already have an agency / I'm locked in a contract"

> "Good — keep them. We're not a marketing company and we'd be a bad one. We work on what happens
> after their lead lands. If anything, their numbers look better because their leads convert
> better. I've had agencies refer us."

### "$9,500 is a lot"

> "It is. It's also less than one accepted case, and the guarantee is five more consults sat a
> month — which at your acceptance rate and case value is about $49,000 a month. If I'm wrong,
> I work free until I'm not. And if you'd rather not commit to the whole thing, there's a smaller
> version I'd rather sell you than nothing."

→ Go to D1.

### "How do I know the extra consults came from you?"

> "You won't have to take my word for it. We agree your baseline in writing before we start —
> your trailing 90-day average of consults that actually sat, from your own records. Everything
> above that number is what you pay performance on. If the number doesn't move, you owe no
> performance fee. It's the cleanest metric in your practice — either someone sat in the chair or
> they didn't."

### "We're too busy right now"

> "That's usually the best time — a busy practice is leaking more, not less. But the honest answer
> is the install needs about three hours of your time across three weeks: 90 minutes at kickoff,
> an hour on camera, and signatures on five lender applications. Everything else is us. If you
> can't find three hours in three weeks, I'd rather we talk in a quarter."

### "Can you do it cheaper?"

> "No, but I can do less. The financing piece on its own is $2,500 one-time, and it was the single
> biggest number on your audit. Start there."

Never discount the main offer. Downsell the scope instead. A discounted client anchors low forever
and tells other dentists your price.

### "Are you US-based?"

Answer directly. Hedging kills the sale.

> "I'm based in India — our delivery team is there, and one reason we can do weekly call reviews
> and 24/7 lead response at this price is that our cost structure is different from a US agency's.
> Anyone on our team who can see patient contact information is named in the BAA, and every message
> goes out from your systems under your practice name. Happy to walk you through exactly who has
> access to what."

Then move on. Confidence, specificity, and the BAA answer handle this. Apologising does not.

---

# After the call

| Timing | Action |
|---|---|
| Within 5 min | Agreement sent (while still warm) |
| Day 1 | If unsigned: one text, no pitch — *"sent the agreement, any questions on it?"* |
| Day 3 | Call. Offer D1 explicitly if main offer stalls |
| Day 7 | Final: *"parking this — want me to check back in 60 days?"* |
| Day 60, 120 | Re-offer with updated benchmark data from your growing audit book |

**Never chase more than four times.** Move them to the benchmark nurture list. About 15% of audit
buyers who don't close initially close within six months, and they close faster because the pain
compounds while your evidence base grows.
