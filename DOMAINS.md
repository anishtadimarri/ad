# DOMAIN RANKING — Model V2

**~4,400 candidates generated and checked against Verisign RDAP.** The brief changed with the model,
so two criteria are new. Generated by [`scoring/domains.py`](scoring/domains.py).

---

## 1. The two new criteria, and why they decide it

### `SAYSPEOPLE` — the video-studio misread is the expensive one

V2 sells **people**. A production studio is a **competitor category.** So every name that sounds
like a studio is disqualified however well it reads — which kills the entire film-craft register
that a creative-talent business would otherwise reach for first:

| Tempting | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Reel · Cut · Splice · Frame · Render · Footage | All name the **output**. A buyer hearing them expects to buy video, not hire a person |
| **Cast · Casting · Callback · Slate · Lineup** | **All name the act of choosing people.** Creative-world vocabulary that is about *hiring*, which is the half we want |

### `FALSIFIABLE` — a claim you can prove beats a claim you assert

V2's positioning is **premium**, from a company with no track record. That makes the *kind* of claim
matter more than its strength:

| | Words | The buyer's reaction |
|---|---|---|
| **Provable** | Graded · Vetted · Merit · Tested | *"Graded how?"* — and we answer with the retention test. **The name creates the sales conversation we want** |
| Asserted | Caliber · Select · Chosen · Sterling · Prime | *"Says you."* Credible from an incumbent, thin from a stranger |
| Superlative | World Class · Elite · Best | **Reads as marketing.** The least believable option is the one that states the claim outright |

## 2. On TLDs: the question turned out to be moot

`.com` is **available for every finalist** — so there is no trust trade-off to make, and `TLD` carries
only 6 of 100 points. For reference, the ordering that would have applied:

| TLD | Trust for a US B2B buyer | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **.com** | **5** — the only one that needs no explanation | **Take it** |
| **.co** | 4 — reads as a company abbreviation | Buy defensively, ~$30 |
| **.net** | 3 — trusted and dated | Skip |
| **.io** | 3 — reads as dev tools, plus the Chagos sovereignty question | Skip |
| **.ai** | 3 in tech circles — and **it misrepresents a business that sells humans** | Actively wrong here |
| **.studio** | 2 — and it says *studio*, the one thing we are not | Never |
| **.team · .work · .agency** | 2 — reads as the domain you bought because the `.com` was gone | Skip |

## 4. The top four, with the argument

**1. Graded Crew** · `gradedcrew.com` · **92.2**  
Warmest of the provable set, and the cleanest register of any candidate. "Crew" is unambiguously people. Weaker on premium — a crew sounds capable rather than exceptional.

**2. Graded Cast** · `gradedcast.com` · **91.4**  
**Casting is the act of choosing creative talent** — a hiring word from the creative world, not a production word, so it never reads as a studio. And **"graded" names the one thing that makes the premium claim provable**: every candidate carries a retention score. Faint podcast echo on "cast" is the only cost.

**3. Merit Roster** · `meritroster.com` · **90.4**  
**Underrated.** "Merit" implies a standard was applied rather than asserted, which is most of what "graded" buys, in a warmer word.

**4. Graded Roster** · `gradedroster.com` · **89.8**  
"Roster" is the client's word for their own team, which is the right side of the table. Faint shift-scheduling echo.

**And the test is unchanged.** A name is a 5% variable; the first line of the ad is a 50% one. These
are cheap enough to hold two and let the first campaign decide — the same two-brand split already
budgeted inside the $1,500 demand test.
