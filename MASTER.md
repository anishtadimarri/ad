## 1. The business in one page

**What it is:** a recruiting business that places full-time offshore professionals into US small
and mid-market companies, charging a one-time fee of ~35% of first-year salary. The worker is
employed directly by the US client. We source, vet, and hand off. We do not employ, we do not
run payroll, we do not carry the headcount.

**The core arbitrage:** a US employer pays $60–115k fully loaded for a role that a comparably
skilled person outside the US will do for $12–30k — while that person earns 2–5x their local
market rate. Both sides win materially. We take a one-time slice.

**The positioning that makes it a media business rather than a relationship business:**

> *"Hire a full-time remote accountant. $20,000/year instead of $95,000.
> Three vetted candidates in 48 hours. Interview them free."*

This is the pivot that matters. Selling "offshore staffing to accounting firms" addresses
~10–15k firms — an ICP concentration of 0.3–0.75% on Meta, which is unbuyable. Selling
"hire a remote accountant" addresses 500k–2M US businesses at ~5% concentration [E], which is
buyable. Same delivery, 40x the buyer universe.

**Target:** $5M revenue in 3–5 years. At ~$7,000 average fee that is **~714 placements/year, or
60/month.** Somewhere does 250/month [V]. So the target is roughly a quarter of one existing
competitor's current run rate.

**Why this and not the other 60 things considered:** it is the only archetype found that
simultaneously clears a 1.5:1 30-day payback on conservative assumptions, has negative working
capital, has real enterprise value at exit (4–8x EBITDA), has infinite slots (not
winner-take-most), and can absorb the founder's actual skill (paid acquisition).

## 2. Decision history — what was killed, and by what evidence

This is the most valuable section in the document. Most of the work done so far was
elimination, and the eliminations are load-bearing: each one is a trap that looks attractive
from the outside.

### 2.1 Killed on unit economics

| Idea | Killed by |
|---|---|
| Consumer subscription apps | 12-month LTV $68.90 vs CAC $72 [V]. Top 10% of apps take 94.5% of revenue [V]. Structurally negative |
| D2C physical products | Median CAC $130–156, gross margin 56.6%, payback 4–24 months, net margin 3–10% [V]. Also violates the no-physical-products constraint |
| Paid communities | 6–9% monthly churn [V]. Revenue per member $29 [V]. 44% never pass 100 members [V] |
| Pay-per-appointment | $150–600 per booked meeting [V] but hidden retainer floors are the norm — the honest version doesn't clear |

### 2.2 Killed by the "upstream benefactor" test

**The test:** if any party profits from the SMB's success, that party gives the education away
free, and your paid version cannot compete. This killed an entire category of training/course
businesses.

| Idea | Who gives it away free |
|---|---|
| HVAC operations templates | ServiceTitan |
| Roofing sales training | GAF, Owens Corning |
| Dental practice courses | Dentsply, implant manufacturers |
| Insurance agent training | IMOs — they fund it out of override commissions |

### 2.3 Killed on price compression (real market prices checked)

| Idea | Assumed price | Actual market price |
|---|---|---|
| Therapist CE subscription | $199/yr | **$74.95–129.99/yr unlimited** [V] — Free State Social Work $74.99, Quantum $74.95, CE4Less $89.99, Aspira $129.99 |
| Property tax appeal packet | $297 | **$49 flat** (AppealDesk, identical AI-generated product) [V]; Ownwell/TaxProper charge **$0 upfront** on 25% contingency [V] |
| US company formation | Recurring | Stripe Atlas / doola price at or below cost as a customer-acquisition loss leader [V] |

### 2.4 Killed on market structure

| Idea | Structural problem |
|---|---|
| Marketplaces | Winner-take-most. Second place is worth ~nothing |
| Productized DFY agency | No structural retention after install. Honest SMB retainer life is **6–12 months**, not the 56 months an earlier model implied. 60–70% of churn happens in the first six months [V] |
| Anything requiring a sales call to clear CAC | Violates the hard constraint. Front end must clear on its own |

## 3. The filter framework

### 3.1 Hard constraints (non-negotiable, set by the operator)

1. **30-day LTGP:CAC ≥ 1.5:1** — gross profit collected within 30 days, over fully loaded CAC
2. **No sales call may determine CAC** — the front end clears on its own. Upsells into an
   already-1.5:1 base are fine; a base that only works because of a closer is not
3. **No physical products**
4. **Buildable from India, sellable anywhere**
5. **Launch within one month**
6. **$5M revenue achievable in 3–5 years**
7. **Real enterprise value at exit,** not just cash flow

### 3.2 Market filters (Hormozi "Starving Crowd")

| Filter | Applied here |
|---|---|
| **Pain** | Is it bleeding, or is it a vitamin? |
| **Purchasing power** | Can the buyer write the cheque without a committee? |
| **Targetability** | Can you reach them on a channel you can operate? |
| **Growing** | Is the market getting bigger or smaller? |

### 3.3 The seven role filters (derived in §7)

Applied to select *which* skill to place:

1. **Async-capable** — no mandatory US-hours voice work
2. **Gradeable** — a work sample with an objectively correct output
3. **Trainable** — a curriculum exists; ramp under 4 weeks
4. **Weak local demand / strong international demand** — the arbitrage survives year two
5. **Deep supply pipeline** — a degree program produces tens of thousands per year
6. **AI-resistant** — judgment and exception handling, not keystrokes
7. **Low client-side management burden** — the deliverable is self-evidently right or wrong

### 3.4 The meta-principle

> **Trainability is the moat.** If quality can be *manufactured* (academy + graded intake)
> rather than *sourced* (skim the top 1%), then quality stops being luck, supply stops being
> scarce, and the training itself becomes a retention mechanism — people don't leave the
> organization that credentialed them.

This single principle explains the entire competitive landscape: Oceans skims and caps at
30–40 placements/month; Somewhere doesn't skim and does 250/month [V]. Manufacturing quality
is how you get both.

## 4. Market structure — offshore staffing and placement

### 4.1 Size and direction

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| US accounting establishments | **137,100** [V] |
| US accounting industry revenue | **$252.3B** [V] |
| US accounting workforce | **1.30M** [V] |
| Firms with <10 employees | **120,000+** [V] |
| Share of firms using offshore staff today | **30–35%** [V] |
| Projected by 2028 | **50%+** [V] |
| Firms taking 60+ days to fill a role | **~50%** [V] |

The direction is the point: this is a market where the *default* is changing. In 2019 offshore
was a cost play for the sophisticated. By 2028 it is the norm. A business launched now is
riding the adoption curve, not creating it.

### 4.2 US compensation — the left side of the arbitrage

| Role | Base | Fully loaded |
|---|---|---|
| Bookkeeper | **$55,893** avg [V] | ~$70–78k [E] |
| Staff accountant | **$65,000** [V] | **$94,000–115,000** [V] |
| Senior accountant | **$79,172** [V] | ~$110–135k [E] |
| Controller | **$152,000–213,250** [V] | **$231,000–259,000** [V] |

"Fully loaded" = base + payroll taxes + benefits + equipment + space + recruiting amortization.
It is roughly **1.45x base** [E]. This multiplier is the single most important number in every
sales conversation, because clients anchor on base and forget the rest.

### 4.4 Attrition — the number that decides whether the promise holds

**By function:**

| Function | Annual attrition |
|---|---|
| Engineering | **12%** [V] — lowest |
| Executive assistant | **<10%** [V]; 93% retained at 12 months across 272 client firms (VirtuHire, South Africa) [C] |
| Operations | **21.3%** [V] — highest of the white-collar functions |
| SDR | **35–40% in year one**; 14–18 month tenure [V] |

**By country:**

| Location | BPO attrition |
|---|---|
| Manila | **40–60%** [V] |
| Cebu | **25–35%** [V] |
| India — ITeS | **11–18%** [V] |
| India — BPO | **30–40%** [V] |

**The pattern:** the split is not country, it is **shift and voice**. Manila's 40–60% is
graveyard-shift voice work. Cebu is the same country at half the attrition because the work mix
differs. India ITeS at 11–18% is async back-office. Any promise about retention is really a
promise about which *kind* of work you place.

### 4.5 Wage inflation — the year-two risk

| Market | Annual salary inflation |
|---|---|
| India BPO | **9.5%** [V] |
| LatAm | Broadly stable [V] |

This is the quiet killer of offshore placements. You place someone at $20k. Two years later the
local market pays $24k for the same skill, a competitor calls them, and the client loses the
person they were told would stay. Consequences designed around this are in §9.4.

### 4.6 Country specialization

| Geography | Strongest for |
|---|---|
| **India** | Bookkeeping, accounting, legal, technical, research, engineering/AEC |
| **Philippines** | Customer-facing, sales, voice, general VA |
| **LatAm** | Software, finance, bilingual CX. **76% of US nearshore users plan more hires** [V] |
| **South Africa** | Native-English async support and EA. Attrition 20–30% [V] |

**Worker-side economics:** a Philippine VA earns ~**$2.05/hr** locally but **2–5x that** working
internationally through an agency [V]. That multiple is the retention mechanism — and it is
also what makes this genuinely win-win rather than extractive.

### 5.4 What the field tells you

1. **There is no winner-take-most dynamic.** TOA, Entigrity, Somewhere, Oceans, Wing, Athena,
   MyOutDesk and hundreds of smaller shops coexist. Slots are effectively infinite because the
   buying unit is one company hiring one person, and no network effect accrues.
2. **Nobody in the category acquires on paid media at scale.** Every scaled player grew on a
   founder audience, referral, or outbound. This is the founder's actual edge and it is
   genuinely untested — which cuts both ways.
3. **Capital efficiency is real** (Wing: $48M valuation on $2.2M raised).
4. **You can copy Somewhere close to dot-for-dot and survive** — but you cannot copy their
   distribution, which was a pre-existing 400k audience. The distribution has to be solved
   differently, and that is the whole risk.

## 6. Business model architecture — the five variants

| Model | Who employs | Revenue shape | Working capital | Liability | Exit multiple |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Recruit-and-handoff** | Client | One-time 25–35% of salary | **Negative** — collect before you spend | Minimal | 4–8x EBITDA, +1–2 turns for placement mix [V] |
| **B. Managed seats / markup** | You (or EOR) | Monthly spread, $500–1,500/seat | **$300–400k payroll float** [E] | Employment liability | 4–7x, more durable revenue |
| **C. EOR** | You, legally | Flat $299–599/employee/mo | Float + compliance reserve | High — you are the employer of record | 6–10x (SaaS-like) |
| **D. BPO / outcome** | You | Per-output or per-FTE | High | Delivery liability | 5–8x |
| **E. Build-Operate-Transfer** | You → client | Setup + monthly → handover | Very high | High | Lumpy, project-shaped |

### 6.1 Why A (recruit-and-handoff)

| Reason | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Negative working capital** | The fee is collected at or before start date. You are funded by customers, not by a lender. Model B requires $300–400k of payroll float before you have a single dollar of profit [E] |
| **No employment liability** | You never fire anyone, never handle a wrongful-termination claim, never owe severance in a jurisdiction you don't understand |
| **Headcount stays flat** | Model B at $5M revenue implies ~300 placed workers on *your* books. Model A implies ~30 people total |
| **Better multiple** | "Placement/conversion fee revenue trades 1–2 EBITDA turns higher" than contract staffing [V] |
| **Launchable in one month** | No entity registration abroad, no payroll infrastructure, no float |

### 6.2 The cost of choosing A

You give up recurring revenue and you give up control of post-placement retention. This is the
central trade and it has two consequences:

1. **The guarantee must be 6-month conditional, not 12-month.** In handoff you do not manage the
   worker, so you cannot underwrite their retention past the point where the client's own
   management becomes the dominant variable.
2. **A protection/subscription layer is needed** to create recurring revenue and to buy back
   *some* retention influence. See §9.3.

### 6.3 The hybrid actually recommended

**Model A as the base, with a thin Model-B-flavoured layer bolted on:**

```
PLACEMENT FEE (one-time, 35%)          ← the business
   + ENGAGEMENT DEPOSIT (credited)      ← pulls cash into the 30-day window
   + PROTECTION SUBSCRIPTION ($297/mo)  ← recurring revenue + retention influence
   + REPLACEMENT GUARANTEE (6-mo cond.) ← risk reversal
```

| **Marketing ops (HubSpot/Marketo)** | Platform certifications — objective, portable | Any company with a marketing stack | $70–90k [E] | Certification-gradeable like Salesforce admin. Async, sticky, and *unglamorous* — low escape risk is a feature |
| **Email / lifecycle (Klaviyo)** | Flow performance is revenue-attributable | Ecom brands, agencies | $60–80k [E] | Quality is a number. Deep demand. Lower escape risk than portfolio-creative roles |
| **Amazon PPC / marketplace ops** | ACoS / TACoS — an objective score | Brands, aggregators, agencies | $55–75k [E] | Highly gradeable, enormous volume, low glamour |

### 7.4 Ranking, on the stated criterion (over-deliver on quality *and* attrition, at volume)

| # | Role | Case |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Steel/rebar detailing + MEP/BIM** | Best gradeability, biggest wage gap, zero shift work, zero seasonality |
| **2** | **Insurance processing** | Best recurring volume, lowest ramp, proven at 10k-employee scale |
| **3** | **Paraplanning (RIA)** | Best academy-as-moat, stickiest client, highest-margin buyer |
| **4** | **Construction estimating** | Best pain — it is a revenue bottleneck, not a cost line |
| **5** | **Bookkeeping / AP-AR / payroll** | Best-understood buyer, most incumbents, easiest to explain |

**Why not EA — the role both incumbents lead with?** EA has the best attrition in the dataset
(<10%) and the highest willingness to pay ($3,000/mo), but it fails filters 2 and 3: quality is
personality, so it cannot be graded pre-placement and cannot be manufactured by training. That
is precisely why Oceans must skim the top 1% and why they cap at 30–40 placements/month. EA is a
great role for a business built on scarcity. It is a bad role for a business built on volume.

### 7.5 The wage-gap comparison (why AEC leads)

| Role | Local alternative for the worker | Remote seat we offer | Multiple |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indian civil engineer / detailer | Site role at ₹25–40k/month [E] | ₹110–150k/month [E] | **3–4x** |
| Indian bookkeeper | ₹30–50k/month, 5 local employers bidding [E] | ₹100–140k/month [E] | 2.5–3x, **but contested** |

The AEC worker has no good local alternative. The bookkeeper does. That difference *is* the
retention promise, and it is structural rather than cultural.

### 7.6 The founder-as-academy exception (paid media)

§3.4 established that trainability is the moat. But trainability has a cost that was never
priced: for AEC you must **buy** the curriculum — hire a senior detailer, build QA from nothing.
For paraplanning you license CFP material. For every role in §7.3 the academy is a year-two
capital project.

**For paid media it already exists, in the founder's head.** The operator can personally grade a
candidate's ad-account audit and personally certify the output. No competitor in the category can
do this — Somewhere can't train media buyers, TOA can't, Oceans can't.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Gradeable** | Strongly — a real account audit, scored. More objective than bookkeeping |
| **Trainable** | Uniquely so. Zero cost to stand up the academy |
| **Supply** | Deep — every Indian performance agency is a talent pool |
| **Demand** | Huge and cross-vertical |
| **Escape risk** | **Highest in the entire taxonomy.** Direct-to-client at 3–5x |
| **AI exposure** | Real. Advantage+/PMax automate the junior execution layer |

**Verdict:** a strong **second role or wedge**, not obviously the base. The quality side is

(where the ladder is visible), or a revenue-share that makes staying more lucrative than leaving.

### 7.7 Omission audit — how marketing was missed

Recorded because the failure mode will recur.

| Cause | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Branch conflation** | "Don't sell marketing services" (a settled decision) leaked into "don't place marketing people" (a different question that was never asked). Primary cause |
| **Inherited category boundaries** | The role map was derived from the competitor teardown (§5) rather than from the economy. Marketing is under-represented among the incumbents, so it was under-represented in the map |
| **Unstated filter** | "Bought as a service, not a seat" was applied silently. It is legitimate but partial — it kills agency-shaped creative work and does **not** kill operational marketing roles |
| **Enumerate-then-drop** | Marketing appeared in the §7.1 taxonomy, then vanished — present in neither the kill table nor the survivors table. This is worse than never enumerating, because the list looks complete |

**Guard for future passes:** every branch in §7.1 must terminate in either §7.2 (killed, with a
reason) or §7.3 (survives). Silent disappearance is a bug.

### 8.1 Decision: go deep on ONE country first

## 9. The recommended model specification

### 9.1 Offer

> **Hire a full-time remote [role]. $20,000/year instead of $95,000.
> Three vetted candidates in 48 hours. Interview them free. Pay nothing until you hire.**

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| **Fee** | **35% of first-year salary**, one-time |
| **Engagement deposit** | $500–1,000, non-refundable, **credited in full against the fee** |
| **Salary transparency** | Client sees the exact salary the worker receives. No hidden spread |
| **Shortlist** | **3 vetted candidates, free, before any commitment** — bench-enabled |
| **Speed** | 48h to shortlist, 7–21 days to start [V, matches Somewhere] |
| **Guarantee** | **6-month conditional free replacement** (conditions in §9.5) |
| **Protection layer** | **$297/month** — see §9.3 |

### 9.2 Why the engagement deposit exists (it is not a money grab)

It solves the single biggest threat to the 30-day payback criterion. Fill takes 7–21 days [V],
plus a sales cycle. Ad-click-to-fee-collected can easily exceed 30 days, which breaks the hard
constraint even though the business is excellent.

The deposit does three things at once:
1. Moves cash inside the 30-day window
2. Filters tyre-kickers, which raises **fill rate** — the core profitability metric (§13.2)
3. Costs the serious buyer nothing, because it is fully credited

### 9.3 The protection layer — $297/month

| Included | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Quarterly performance check-in with the worker | Early warning on disengagement |
| Annual raise administration (see §9.4) | Removes the awkward conversation from the client |
| Priority replacement (48h shortlist, no fee) | Converts the guarantee from a liability into a product |
| Backup bench identified and warm | The actual value — the client is never exposed |
| Payroll/remittance coordination | Removes real friction |

**Attach rate assumption: 30% [?]** — completely unvalidated and one of the top three risks.

### 9.4 The retention architecture (win-win-win)

The client's actual fear is not price. It is: *"I'll invest three months training this person and
they'll leave."* Every design decision below exists to make that fear unfounded.

| Mechanism | Who it protects |
|---|---|
| **Published 9% annual raise** for placed workers, administered by us | Directly neutralizes the 9.5% India wage-inflation counter-bid [V]. The worker's salary tracks the market *without them having to leave to get it* |
| **Transparent salary** — client knows exactly what the worker earns | Removes the worker's #1 grievance in the seat-rental model (discovering the agency takes 60%) |
| **Role selection = async only** | Eliminates the largest attrition driver in the dataset |
| **Academy + credential** | People don't leave the org that certified them; also the quality mechanism |
| **Visible ladder** (Associate → Senior → Lead, with named criteria) | Kills the "dead-end job" driver |
| **Peer cohort** across clients | Kills the isolation driver of solo embedded seats |
| **Client onboarding standards** enforced at intake | The client is often the cause of attrition; refusing bad clients protects the stat |

**The 9% raise commitment is the sharpest single idea in the model** and it is nearly free: the
client pays it (not us), the worker's real wage keeps pace, the counter-bid loses its edge, and
we get an annual touchpoint that justifies the protection subscription.

### 9.5 Guarantee conditions (protect the guarantee or it eats you)

Free replacement within 6 months, **provided**:
1. The client completed the documented onboarding (checklist signed at week 1)
2. The worker was paid on time every cycle
3. The role scope did not materially change from the intake spec
4. The client raised the issue within 14 days of it arising

Without these, a handoff-model guarantee is an unpriced insurance policy on someone else's
management quality.

## 11. Acquisition

### 11.1 The honest position on Meta

**Nobody in this category has made Meta work at scale.** That is a fact, and it has two readings:

- **Bear:** the category is relationship-driven, trust-gated, and low-frequency. Paid social
  can't carry it.
- **Bull:** every scaled player was founded by someone with a pre-existing audience or an
  outbound motion, so paid social was never the cheapest channel *for them*. It has not been
  tested by someone whose primary skill is paid acquisition.

Both are true. The bull case is only available because the broadened positioning (§1) lifted
ICP concentration from **0.3–0.75% to ~5%** [E]. At 0.75% Meta is arithmetically impossible; at
5% it is merely unproven.

### 11.2 Why the broadened hook works

> *"Looking to hire a remote accountant at $20,000 USD, placed within two weeks?"*

It cuts across every vertical simultaneously. It does not require identifying accounting firms;
it requires identifying *anyone who employs an accountant*, which is a far larger and far more
addressable set. The same structure re-skins for every role in §7.3.

### 11.3 Channel portfolio (the user's stated position: "I'll do anything required")

| Channel | Expected CAC | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Meta** | $2,200 [E] | The thesis. Test first because it is fastest to falsify |
| **Cold email** | $1,200 [E] | Reliable in this category. Requires deliverability infrastructure |
| **Referral fees to accountants/consultants** | $500 [E] | Referrals cost **$25/lead vs $497 industry average** [V] and drive **60–80% of revenue for sub-$5M agencies** [V]. Structurally the best channel and the slowest to build |
| **LinkedIn organic + founder content** | Low, slow | This is how every competitor actually did it |
| **Partnerships** (fractional CFO firms, MSPs, franchise systems) | Variable | One partner can supply continuous intake |

### 11.4 The supply-side correction

An early assumption that candidate acquisition would be near-free was wrong. Real costs:
**PH median CPL ₱1,540 (~$27)** [V]; **India ₹400–800/application** [V]; and finance, real estate
and BPO are currently the *most expensive* niches in the Philippines [V]. Supply-side media is a
real line item ($200/placement in §10.1), not a rounding error. Referral and academy pipelines
are the way to drive it down.
