domains move.

## 1. The filters, before any candidates

The name has a specific job here, and it is not "sound nice." It has to survive a cold Meta ad
shown to a skeptical US business owner by a company with **no Western credibility and no track
record** ([`MASTER.md`](MASTER.md) §1). That produces seven filters:

| # | Filter | Why it comes from the model, not from taste |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Spellable from audio** | Primary creative is a screen-recorded Reel ([`MAP.md`](MAP.md)). If they hear it and can't type it, the click is lost |
| **2** | **Not boxed into bookkeeping** | Five roles ship, including an ads manager on a different spine. `LedgerCo` kills role 5 on day one |
| **3** | **Not boxed into placement** | 37–65% of lifetime GP is the recurring EOR layer (§10.5). A name meaning "recruiter" makes the second product a non-sequitur |
| **4** | **Reads as an established firm, not a startup** | The credibility gap is the constraint. Coined words (`Zynta`, `Talentix`) read as new, which is the one thing we cannot afford to read as |
| **5** | **Carries meaning in 3 seconds** | Somewhere and Oceans can be abstract because they have traction. A cold ad from an unknown must say what it is *in the name* |
| **6** | **No "cheap offshore" signal** | The offer is salary transparency and graded work, not arbitrage-as-pitch. No `India`, no `Offshore`, no `Save` |
| **7** | **Bare `.com`** | A `.io` or `.ai` on a B2B service page reads as a side project. This is the filter that eliminated ~95% of candidates |

**662 candidates checked. 322 `.com`s free — almost all of them free because they are bad.**
Every strong two-word `.com` in the category is registered: `cleardesk`, `clearseat`, `steadydesk`,
`steadybench`, `northdesk`, `truedesk`, `openbench`, `benchworks`, `rosterly`, `seatly`,
`ledgerly`, `tallyworks` — all taken.

## 2. What the competitors are named, and what it tells us

| Company | Domain | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Somewhere | `somewhere.com` | Abstract warm noun. Was **Support Shepherd** — renamed *after* traction |
| Oceans | **`oceanstalent.com`** | Abstract warm noun — and **could not get the bare `.com`** |
| Pearl, Wing, Shepherd | single word | Same convention |
| Hey Foster, More Staffing, Virtustant, 1840 & Co | mixed | No convention below the top tier |

**Two things follow.** First, the category convention is *abstract warm noun* — which works once
you have proof and fails cold, which is why filter 5 outranks convention here. Second, **Oceans,
one of the two direct comps, runs on a suffixed `.com`.** So `[brand]talent.com` is category-native
if the bare word is worth it. It usually isn't.

## 3. The recommendation

# Steady Roster · `steadyroster.com`

`.com`, `.co` and `.io` all free as of the check date.

| Why | |
|---|---|
| **It names the largest lever in the model** | Seat continuity is worth **+$9,022 per client** going from 9 to 30 EOR months — 18x what halving CAC adds (§10.5.1). The company is named after the thing that makes it valuable |
| **"Roster" is the buyer's word, not ours** | A *bench* is our inventory; a *desk* is a function; a **roster is their team**. The only one of the three framed from the client's side |
| **It survives both products** | Placement fills the roster, EOR keeps it steady. Neither product is an afterthought in the name |
| **It survives all five roles** | Nothing in it says finance, so the ads manager fits |
| **It names the felt pain, not the mechanism** | Back-office churn is what the buyer actually hates. "Steady" is the promise; offshore is the method, and methods don't belong in names |
| **Passes filter 1 cleanly** | Two common words, one spelling each, 12 characters |
| **Collision risk low** | No company found at that name. Nearest neighbours are generic — *Steady HR Staffing*, *Steady Staffing Solutions*. Different names, different markets, no mark to clear around |

**The honest con:** "roster" carries a faint shift-scheduling / sports connotation, so a buyer could
half-expect software. The ad and the landing page kill that ambiguity in one line, and no candidate
tested was free of a comparable cost.

**It writes copy.** *"Your back office on an even footing."* · *"A roster that stays."* ·
*"Steady Roster fills the seat — and keeps filling it."* The guarantee and the name are the
same sentence, which is the tell that the name is aligned with the model rather than bolted on.

## 5. Killed, with the reason

| Rejected | Filter it failed |
|---|---|
| `filledseat.com` *(free)* | **5.** "Seat filler" is an established entertainment-industry term for someone paid to occupy a seat at an awards show. The search results are all about that |
| `staffedledger.com`, `filledledger.com`, `uprightledger.com` *(free)* | **2.** Boxes into finance and kills the ads manager |
| `steadydesktalent.com`, `clearbenchtalent.com`, `openbenchtalent.com` *(free)* | **7 + 4.** A suffix is only worth paying for a *great* bare word. `Steady Desk` is not one |
| `sedari`, `tenura`, `perdura`, `rostra`, `plenum` | **4 + 7.** Coined and Latinate reads as a startup, and every bare `.com` is taken anyway |
| Anything containing `India`, `Offshore`, `Global`, `Save`, `Cost` | **6.** Leads with the method and invites the price objection before the value one |
| `Somewhere`/`Oceans`-style abstract single words | **5.** Requires traction we don't have. Revisit at $5M, when a rename is an asset rather than a bet — exactly what Somewhere did |

## 6. Entity ≠ brand

Worth separating, because they have different requirements:

| | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Trading brand** | `Steady Roster` — everything above |
| **India Pvt Ltd** | Can be anything MCA will approve. Nobody outside a contract ever reads it |
| **US contracting entity** | A US LLC is what makes a US buyer comfortable signing and makes Stripe/Mercury straightforward. **This is a credibility mechanism, not a tax one** — it belongs in the launch plan next to the guarantee |

Buy `.com` + `.co` + `.io` and the matching handles in one sitting. It is ~$50 and the alternative
is discovering at month three that the name is unusable.
