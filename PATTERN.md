# THE BOOKKEEPING PATTERN — Occupations The US Stopped Producing

Every screen used so far has been **demand-side**: how big is the market, can I reach the buyer,
will they pay. This is a **supply-side** screen, and it is sharper — because when a country stops
producing a worker, the shortage does not respond to price, and the buyer has no local alternative
at any wage.

Evidence tags per MASTER §0.

---

## 1. The pattern, stated precisely

Bookkeeping works as an offshore placement not because it is a big market but because of five
conditions holding simultaneously:

| # | Condition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Structural supply collapse, not cyclical demand** | The pipeline is broken, so the shortage persists through a downturn. Cyclical shortages disappear exactly when you need revenue |
| **2** | **Fully digital, fully async** | No physical presence, no real-time requirement, no night shift |
| **3** | **Decades of offshore precedent** | The buyer is not being asked to believe something new. This is the difference between selling and evangelising |
| **4** | **Portable credential** | The qualification exists abroad, so supply can be verified rather than trusted |
| **5** | **The US wage is high from scarcity, not value** | Bookkeeping is not intrinsically worth $70k. It is worth that because nobody will do it. **That gap is the whole margin** |

**The screen:** *which occupations has the United States stopped producing, where the work is
digital, and where somebody has already proved it transfers?*

That list is short — under a dozen — which is exactly what makes it useful.

## 3. The strongest non-accounting match — insurance

**Insurance is a worse shortage than accounting, and it is not close.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Workers lost to attrition by 2026 | **~400,000** (BLS projection) [V] |
| Retirements reshaping the industry | **1.4 million** [V] |
| Underwriters over 50 | **1 in 4**; average insurance professional is **mid-50s** [V] |
| Share of workforce retiring this decade | **~half**, taking tacit knowledge with them [V] |
| **Millennials considering an insurance career** | **4%** [V] ← the pipeline collapse, worse than accounting's |

**Roles inside it that fit the pattern:** underwriting support · policy checking · endorsement
processing · COI issuance · claims processing · loss-run analysis · premium audit · **actuarial
analysis** (explicitly named in the retirement wave).

| Pattern condition | Insurance |
|---|---|
| 1. Structural collapse | ✅ **Stronger than accounting** |
| 2. Digital and async | ✅ Fully — processing work needs no live overlap |
| 3. Offshore precedent | ✅ **ReSource Pro ~10,000 employees**, plus WNS, EXL, Genpact — 20+ years |
| 4. Portable credential | ✅ No US licence needed for *processing* (licensing applies to advice, not admin) |
| 5. Wage from scarcity | ✅ Underwriters are paid for judgment that nobody is being trained to acquire |

**The catch, and it is real:** insurance scored `META` = 3 and `WHITE` = 1 in the vertical model —
agency owners are reachable but not Meta-native, and ReSource Pro already dominates the category at
~10,000 employees. **Best pattern match, worst competitive position.** That is a genuine trade, not
a detail.

## 4. Everything else that fits the pattern

| Rank | Occupation | Structural shortage evidence | Offshore precedent | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Insurance underwriting / policy servicing / claims** | ~400,000 attrition; 4% of millennials interested [V] | ReSource Pro, WNS, EXL — 20+ yrs | ReSource Pro dominates; buyer not Meta-native |
| **2** | **Structural steel detailing · CAD · BIM · MEP** | High-skilled manufacturing workers average **age 57**; 97% of firms worried about brain drain, half "very concerned" [V] | Mature Indian industry — Outsource2India, IndiaCADWorks and dozens more, decades old | **Buyer is conservative** — this is what killed AEC in earlier rounds (`RR` = 2–3) |
| **3** | **Medical coding · clinical documentation** | Documented coder shortage; AAPC credential | Enormous — Indian RCM is a multi-billion industry | Most contested category in existence (`WHITE` = 0–1) |
| **4** | **Actuarial analysis** | Named explicitly in the insurance retirement wave [V] | WNS, EXL do actuarial work | Small volume; long credential runway |
| **5** | **Pharmacovigilance · clinical data management · regulatory affairs** | Life-sciences pipeline shortage; heavily regulated so AI-durable | **India is the global hub** — largest concentration worldwide | Buyer is pharma/CRO — **enterprise, not SMB** |
| **6** | **Title examination / abstracting** | Aging workforce, thin pipeline | Established | Rate-cyclical — fails condition 1 in a downturn |
| **7** | **Payroll & benefits administration** | Documented shortage; compliance complexity rising | Established | Modest fee, narrow scope |
| **8** | **Patent / IP support** — docketing, searching, illustration | Patent agent shortage; specialised | Real Indian patent-support industry | Small volume |

### What deliberately fails the pattern

| Occupation | Fails on |
|---|---|
| Nurses · truck drivers · HVAC techs · dental hygienists · electricians | **Condition 2** — physical presence. The biggest US shortages are un-offshorable, which is exactly why they are shortages |
| Software engineering | **Condition 5** — the US wage is high because the work is *valuable*, not scarce. So the arbitrage is 54% (Somewhere's figure) rather than 78%, and the local counter-bid in India is fierce |
| Cybersecurity / SOC | **Condition 2** — 24/7 shift coverage |
| Mortgage processing · solar design | **Condition 1** — cyclical, not structural |
| Data entry · transcription · basic content | AI. There is no shortage of a thing a model does for free |
| Executive assistant | **Condition 4** — no portable credential, so quality cannot be verified. Savings are 76% but the work is ungradeable |

## 5. What the supply-side screen says that the demand-side screens missed

**Ranked purely on pattern strength, ignoring channel and competition:**

1. Insurance underwriting and policy servicing
2. Accounting and bookkeeping *(the reference)*
3. Steel detailing / CAD / BIM
4. Medical coding

**Ranked on the full picture including Meta reachability, whitespace and buyer readiness:**

1. **Accounting and bookkeeping** — the only one that scores well on *both* screens
2. Insurance — best pattern, weakest position
3. Medical coding — best precedent, worst competition
4. Steel detailing — best arbitrage, hardest buyer

> **The reference case wins on the combined view, which is a reassuring result rather than a
> circular one:** bookkeeping is not the strongest pattern match — insurance is — but it is the
> only occupation that is simultaneously a genuine structural shortage, fully async, decades
> proven, credential-verifiable, *and* attached to buyers who are Meta-reachable and not yet owned
> by a 10,000-person incumbent.

### The one genuinely new idea this screen produces

**Insurance policy servicing sold to e-commerce-style buyers is not available — but insurance
*agencies* are one of the few verticals where the shortage is so acute that `RR` and `META` may not
matter.** When 4% of millennials will consider your industry and half your workforce retires this
decade, the buyer stops needing to be persuaded that remote works. They need somebody, anybody,
who can process an endorsement.

That is worth a test: **five conversations with independent agency owners** before dismissing
insurance on channel grounds. It costs a week and the pattern data says it is the largest
structural shortage in any offshorable white-collar occupation in the US.
