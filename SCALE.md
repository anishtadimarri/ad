## 1. The gaps, ranked by severity *at $100M*

The reframe that matters: **gaps that are survivable at $3M are fatal at $100M.** Ranked for the
larger ambition, not the launch.

### Gap 1 — $100M is arithmetically impossible on placement fees ⛔

| Revenue per placement | Placements needed | Per month | vs Somewhere |
|---|---|---|---|
| $6,000 (fee only) | 16,667/yr | 1,389 | **5.6x their entire volume** |
| $8,829 (fee + EOR) | 11,326/yr | 944 | **3.8x their entire volume** |

Somewhere does 250 placements/month across all roles and 18+ countries after four-plus years [V].
Recruiting firms that reach $100M+ (Robert Half and peers) run on **contract hours billed**, not
one-time placement fees. **Placement fee revenue does not scale to $100M. Full stop.**

### Gap 2 — AI is compressing the exact role being sold ⛔

Bookkeeping scored **2/5 on AI durability** in the role model (MASTER §7). Over a ten-year build
that is existential — *if* you sell seats. See §5: it inverts into an advantage if you sell
outcomes. But on the current model it is the single largest long-term threat.

### Gap 3 — the arbitrage has a half-life ⚠️

India BPO salary inflation runs **9.5%/yr** [V] against US wage growth of ~3–4% [E]. Compounded:

| | Today | +5 yrs | +10 yrs |
|---|---|---|---|
| India salary ($20,000 base) | $20,000 | $31,500 | $49,600 |
| US loaded equivalent ($95,000) | $95,000 | $112,000 | $131,000 |
| **Spread** | **4.8x** | **3.6x** | **2.6x** |

The pitch weakens every year on its own. You are riding a closing window, which argues for
building the recurring layers *now* rather than after the placement business matures.

### Gap 4 — no recurring revenue ⚠️

Even at 70% EOR attach, recurring is **38% of lifetime GP**. A $100M business with rich margins
needs 70%+. Placement fee revenue trades at 4–8x EBITDA; recurring trades at 8–12x.

### Gap 5 — 1 worker : 1 client caps margin structurally ⚠️

Staff augmentation is locked at one worker per client, so revenue per worker is capped at whatever
one seat bills. **This is the real ceiling on margin, and it is a model choice rather than an
operational problem.** See §3.

### Gap 6 — no moat

The offer improvements (12-month unlimited guarantee, free graded shortlist, work-sample test) are
copyable in a week by a competitor with 250 placements/month of proof. No network effects. The
only compounding asset is the assessment-to-outcome dataset (MASTER §9.6.3), and that needs ~100
placements before it means anything.

### Gap 7 — three concentration risks

One niche (e-comm), one geography (India), one channel. Each is correct for the wedge and each is
a single point of failure at scale.

### Gap 8 — zero proof

No customers. Every number is [E] or [?]. This is gap #1 for the next 90 days and gap #8 for the
ten-year plan.

## 2. The reframe

> **Placement fees are the customer-acquisition engine, not the business.**
>
> You acquire a client at a **$4,091 profit** (OFFER §4) and then sell them **$30,000/year of
> recurring service.** Negative CAC on a recurring revenue base. That is a fundamentally better
> business than recruiting, and it is only reachable *through* recruiting.

Nobody in this category has built it this way. TOA and Entigrity sell seats. Somewhere sells
placements. Deel sells EOR. **Nobody sells the outcome to the client the placement acquired.**

## 3. Revenue per customer by rung — the whole argument

| Rung | Revenue/yr | Gross margin | Workers per client | Recurring |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Placement fee | $6,000 | 54% | 1:1 | ❌ one-time |
| EOR / payroll ($399/mo) | $4,788 | **72%** | 1:1 | ✅ |
| Managed seat ($2,300/mo) | $27,600 | 38% | 1:1 | ✅ |
| **Managed SERVICE ($2,000/mo × 3 clients per accountant)** | **$72,000** | **65%** | **1:3** | ✅ |
| Vertical software ($299/mo) | $3,588 | **85%** | n/a | ✅ |

**The unlock is the fourth row.** A managed seat bills one client per worker at a 38% margin
because you are reselling a salary. A managed **service** runs one accountant across three small
e-comm clients — the work genuinely fits, because a $3–8M brand needs perhaps 12 hours a week of
real accounting, not 40.

**Three clients per accountant at $2,000/month each = $72,000 of revenue on ~$25,000 of loaded
cost.** That is 65% gross margin on recurring revenue, versus 38% for seat rental and 54% one-time
for placement.

**This single structural change is where "rich margins" come from.** Not pricing, not scale, not
negotiation — the worker-to-client ratio.

| | |

Because revenue per client is 5x higher, the transaction count stops being the constraint.

## 5. Why AI inverts from threat to advantage

This is the most important strategic point in the document.

| If you sell… | AI does this | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Seats** (staff aug, EOR) | Client needs fewer accountants | **Revenue falls. You are destroyed** |
| **Outcomes** (managed service) | One accountant serves 5 clients instead of 3 | **Margin rises. You get richer** |

Under a managed service, AI raises the worker-to-client ratio, and every point of that ratio drops
straight to gross margin:

| Clients per accountant | Revenue per accountant | Gross margin |
|---|---|---|
| 3 (today, manual) | $72,000 | 65% |
| 4 | $96,000 | **74%** |
| 5 (AI-assisted) | $120,000 | **79%** |

**So the managed service is not only the higher-margin model — it is the AI hedge.** Gap 2 becomes
Gap 2-shaped upside, but only under the outcome model. This is the strongest single argument for
making the pivot early rather than late.

## 6. The sequence

Each stage is funded by the one before it. No outside capital required until stage 4, and even
then only for the own-entity EOR.

| Stage | Years | Build | Revenue | Recurring | The proof point |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 0–1.5 | **Placement only.** One role, one country, one ICP | $0.5–3M | <10% | Fill rate >60%, CAC <$900 |
| **2** | 1.5–3 | **White-label EOR** bolted onto every placement | $3–8M | 30–40% | EOR attach >45%, churn <2%/mo |
| **3** | 3–5 | **Managed service launch.** The pivot. Sell the close, not the seat | $8–25M | 55–65% | 3 clients per accountant sustained |
| **4** | 5–7 | **Own-entity EOR** + close-automation software | $25–60M | 75%+ | Margin above 65% blended |
| **5** | 7–10 | **Second vertical, second geography** | $60–100M | 85%+ | Repeatability outside e-comm |

**Stage 3 is the whole game.** Stages 1–2 are a good $8M business. Stage 3 is what makes it a
$100M business, and it is a genuine pivot: different pricing, different delivery, different
quality model, different sales motion. Do not drift into it — plan it.

### What each stage must not do

| Stage | The trap |
|---|---|
| 1 | Adding roles or countries before fill rate is proven. Narrowness *is* the offer |
| 2 | Owning entities too early. White-label until the line is proven (MASTER §9.6, OFFER §4) |
| 3 | Running the managed service on the same P&L as placement. It has different unit economics and will be judged wrongly |
| 4 | Building software before 500+ clients are on one workflow. Otherwise you are guessing |
| 5 | Expanding geography and vertical simultaneously |

## 7. What would kill it

| Risk | Kills which stage | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Fill rate below 40%** | 1 | Deposit gates the search, bench, narrow role |
| **Meta/cold email CAC above $414** | 1 | Partnerships and referral, which are cheaper anyway |
| **EOR attach below 25%** | 2 | Make EOR the default rather than the upsell |
| **Managed service margin below 50%** | 3 | Enforce the 1:3 ratio. Below 1:2.5 the model is just seat rental |
| **Client refuses to buy outcomes, insists on "their" person** | 3 | ⚠️ **The real risk.** Run both: seat rental for those who want a person, managed service for those who want a result |
| **India wage inflation outruns pricing** | 4–5 | Second geography, and the 1:N ratio absorbs wage growth |
| **AI collapses the role faster than the ratio can rise** | 4–5 | The ratio *is* the hedge — but only under the outcome model |
| **A funded competitor copies the ladder** | 3–5 | Nobody in the category currently does. Assume 3 years of clear air, not 10 |

**The one to watch is the managed-service objection.** The entire $100M case rests on clients
buying an *outcome* rather than a *person*, and the emotional appeal of "my accountant, on my
team" is exactly what makes the placement offer convert. Those two pitches are in tension, and
the transition is the highest-risk moment in the plan.

## 8. The honest summary

| | |
|---|---|
| **As a placement business** | Caps at $3–5M in this niche, $10–15M across several. 54% GM, 25–30% EBITDA, 4–8x exit |
| **As a placement + EOR business** | $15–30M. 60% GM, 30% EBITDA, 6–8x exit |
| **As a vertical managed service that uses placement as CAC** | **$100M. 67% GM, 32–37% EBITDA, 8–10x exit** |

The wedge does not change. The offer in [`OFFER.md`](OFFER.md) is still what you build first, and
still what you launch in the next thirty days. **What changes is that you build it knowing the
placement fee is the acquisition cost of a recurring-revenue client — not the product.**

That single framing decision determines whether this is a $5M business or a $100M one, and it
costs nothing to adopt today.
