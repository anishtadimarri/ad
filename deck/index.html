Who buys

Internet businesses with real revenue, an owner who decides, a recurring creative workload, and a decision-maker reachable on Meta. Marketing agencies and e-commerce brands at launch.

Why they buy

Not cheap labour — reduced hiring risk plus access to talent they cannot efficiently find themselves. The person becomes their employee and works inside their existing team.

Why it can be won

India is genuinely world-leading in this craft, and nobody currently sells an Indian performance editor to a US agency as a direct hire.

The entire margin is three dimensions

Truth of the claim, gradeability, and whitespace — the three things that decide whether a company with no track record makes a first sale at all.

Compounding, durability and ad legibility — the three that decide whether the business is still good in year three. Hence the second leg at month six.

03The wedge

India is not cheaper at this. It is better at it.

This is the only place in the study where the pitch can be quality rather than price — which matters because a price pitch invites both the price objection and the “why India” objection, and a quality pitch answers both.

Verified supply-side claims

  • Mumbai is the largest VFX hub on earth by headcount
  • Six of the ten fastest-growing VFX and animation hubs globally are Indian
  • 70% of Indian VFX studio revenue is export — Hollywood, Netflix, Amazon
  • DNEG holds eight Academy Awards for Best Visual Effects since 2011
  • More Indians work on VFX in Hollywood films than in Indian films

The arbitrage is against an invoice, not a salary

Buyers under $10M do not employ an editor — they pay a studio monthly. That is the comparator the ad has to beat.

04The offer

Four steps, and the money moves twice

The fragile step in any cold-traffic placement business is the moment a stranger first pays. This offer attacks it twice — once by giving proof away free, once by making the first payment a delivered product rather than a deposit.

01 Free graded shortlist on the call Two or three real candidates, already scored. Turns “trust me” into “look at these three people” $90/call
02 Paid ad-account & creative teardown Their own ads reviewed and scored, delivered. Opens the search — and it is the work sample $500
03 Placement — the person becomes their employee Sourcing, screening, assessment, matching. 12-month unlimited replacement, no cash refunds 30% of comp
04 Employer of record, presented as the default We employ, they manage. Provident fund and gratuity are retention the state enforces $499/mo

Why a paid teardown beats a refundable deposit

“Buy an audit of your ad account” is a smaller decision than “wire me $500 and trust me.” It carries no refund liability, it is revenue in month one, and they own something even if they never hire.

What “premium” means, measurably

One test: recut an existing ad, then measure 3-second and 15-second retention against the original. Gradeable by the operator, publishable as proof, and it doubles as the teardown deliverable.

Cheaper sourcing is worth $169

A standing bench cuts candidate media and grading. It moves the ratio by 0.2. Recruitment is already capped at one month of salary, so there is little to win.

A longer seat is worth $9,015

53× more. And the mechanism is the same graded bench — used for continuity rather than for cost. Without one, a resignation empties the seat and the EOR fee stops.

So build the directory, not an academy

A Slack, a graded roster, and the retention score already produced for every candidate. Free to join, graded on entry, no curriculum. The asset is the list.

Everything survives except two things

Salary, fee, EOR attach and even a doubled CPM all clear comfortably. Only the take-up rate on the first payment is genuinely fragile — halve it and the ratio halves with it.

And the pricing sentence must be unambiguous

A 30% markup on $1,650 a month is $536. A 30% one-time fee on $19,800 is $5,940. The first fails the hard constraint outright; the second is the business. One-time, on first-year compensation.

14What is not yet known

Three tests, under $2,000, before any real spend

Everything on the previous thirteen plates is a model. None of it has met a buyer. These three convert the top of it into fact.

01 Count the postings “Video editor” vs “performance video editor” vs “creative editor,” across agency and e-commerce job boards. Settles whether the intent seed can even be built — and whether the seat is hired often enough to fund a funnel free · 1 day
02 Match-rate test Enrich 2,000 companies that posted the role, upload as a Meta custom audience, read the matched size. Everything about the intent seed depends on this number ~$200 · same day
03 Buy ten held calls Measure cost per held call and teardown take-up — the one fragile input. Count how many buyers ask where the person is based, and how many ask whether it is a shared team ~$1,500 · 10 days

The number that decides the launch

Teardown take-up. 24% gives 6.06:1. At 12% it is 3.03:1 — still above the floor, with nothing to spare. It is the only assumption in the model with no benchmark behind it.

The number that decides the ceiling

Seat life. 9 to 30 months is worth $9,015 per client and cuts the client count for $10M by more than a third. It is won with a contract clause and bench discipline, not with spend.

The honest status

Pre-launch. No customer met, no placement made, no ad run. The evidence base is verified market data and a costed model — not traction.
