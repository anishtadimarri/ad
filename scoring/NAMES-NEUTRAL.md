# Names, Ranked on What the Name Has to Survive

> Criteria rebuilt from first principles, ownership excluded, and **"sounds like a legit
> business" added — which turns out to be the heaviest weight, because it is the operator's
> actual binding constraint.**

---

## 1. Where the criteria come from

Not a list of nice properties. A list of the moments this name has to survive, given the
real position: **unknown, India-based, solo, no track record, selling a $6,000 one-time fee
to US and UK buyers off cold Meta ads.**

| | The moment | The criterion it forces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | It appears in a Meta feed for about a second and a half | `SPOKEN` |
| 2 | **A stranger wires $6,000 to an Indian company they had not heard of that morning** | **`LEGIT`** |
| 3 | They google it before paying | `CLEAR` |
| 4 | It gets said on a call and spelled into a voicemail | `SPOKEN` |
| 5 | It must not force a rebrand at month 18 | `CLEAR` |
| 6 | The business changes — the positioning has already changed **eight times** | `SURVIVES` |
| 7 | It has to be buyable today | `BUYABLE` |

**What did not survive the derivation:** *register* — whether a name sounds evocative like
Somewhere or commodity like Skillforce. That was my aesthetic preference, not a job the name
has to do. Where it genuinely matters, it matters **through** `LEGIT`.

| Criterion | Weight | |
|---|---|---|
| `LEGIT` | 22 | Moment 2. **Does it sound like an incorporated business a finance person would wire $6,000 to** — or like a side project? Absorbs the TLD signal |
| `CLEAR` | 22 | Moments 3 and 5. No operating company or trademark in the hiring trade class, and a search finds *us* |
| `PLAIN` | 18 | **Moment 1½ — does the buyer know the word at all?** Measured from corpus frequency, not judged. Compounds scored on their parts |
| `SURVIVES` | 14 | Moment 6. Method-neutral and role-neutral — the business will move |
| `SPOKEN` | 12 | Moments 1 and 4. Say it, spell it, hear it. No accidental word inside it |
| `APT` | 8 | Does it say something true about the business? **Demoted** — chasing this is what produced the obscure shortlist |
| `BUYABLE` | 4 | Moment 7. Can it be registered today |

**The TLD is folded into `LEGIT` rather than scored separately.** Evidence: `.com` is typed
**~6x more** than `.co` when people guess, and scores **44% memorability against 33%** [V] —
but **no published study isolates the extension as a conversion cause**, so it is a modest
signal inside legitimacy, not a line of its own.

## 3. The top five, in full

### 1. **Standard Talent** — 88  ·  `.co`

**The word is everyday — 95 per million, the most common in the study** — and it still carries institutional weight: Standard Chartered, Standard & Poor's. *We set the standard* is also true of the business. No collision found. Only `.co` is free, because `standardtalent.com` is gone like every other plain word

### 2. **Proof Talent** — 82  ·  `.co`

**Proof is 27 per million — nineteen times more common than *crucible*** — and it means exactly what the page does: we publish the proof. Slightly method-flavoured, which is the only thing holding it back. No collision found

### 3. **All Hands Talent** — 79  ·  `.com`

**Scored with no credit for being owned.** Method-neutral and expands everywhere, but *all hands* is a casual idiom — it reads friendly-agency rather than incorporated, which is the wrong direction for moment 2

### 4. **Merit Talent** — 78  ·  `.co`

Common, clean, says the right thing. *Merit* is used widely enough in the category that the search result will be muddy

### 5. **Harbor Talent** — 78  ·  `.co`

Plain, warm, method-neutral. Says less about the business than the three above

## 4. What adding `LEGIT` did

It reordered the middle of the table, and the reason is worth stating.

| Name | Before | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Top Drawer** | 2nd | 7th | A lovely idiom that reads **consumer boutique**, on a `.co`. Wrong signal for someone about to send money abroad |
| **Sure Hands** · **Handiwork** | 5th · 7th | 13th · 18th | Both read as artisan or home-service brands. Charming, and not what a CFO wires to |
| **Handpicked** | 11th | 16th | The most apt single word found, on the TLD that most says *side project* |
| **Crucible Talent** | 1st | **1st** | Unchanged. It was already the only name scoring well on both meaning and weight |

The gap is **9 points**, and it is concentrated in `APT` and `LEGIT`. *All Hands*

> **This ranking still does not price switching cost.** That was excluded on instruction,
