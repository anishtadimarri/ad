## 1. Where the criteria come from

Not a list of nice properties. A list of the moments this name has to survive, given the
real position: **unknown, India-based, solo, no track record, selling a $6,000 one-time fee
to US and UK buyers off cold Meta ads.**

| | The moment | The criterion it forces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | It appears in a Meta feed for about a second and a half | `SPOKEN` |
| 2 | **A stranger wires $6,000 to an Indian company they had not heard of that morning** | **`LEGIT`** |
| 3 | They google it before paying | `CLEAR` |
| 4 | It gets said on a call and spelled into a voicemail | `SPOKEN` |
| 5 | It must not force a rebrand at month 18 | `CLEAR` |
| 6 | The business changes — the positioning has already changed **eight times** | `SURVIVES` |
| 7 | It has to be buyable today | `BUYABLE` |

**What did not survive the derivation:** *register* — whether a name sounds evocative like
Somewhere or commodity like Skillforce. That was my aesthetic preference, not a job the name
has to do. Where it genuinely matters, it matters **through** `LEGIT`.

| Criterion | Weight | |
|---|---|---|
| `LEGIT` | 26 | Moment 2. **Does it sound like an incorporated business a finance person would wire $6,000 to** — or like a side project? Absorbs the TLD signal |
| `CLEAR` | 24 | Moments 3 and 5. No operating company or trademark in the hiring trade class, and a search finds *us* |
| `SURVIVES` | 18 | Moment 6. Method-neutral and role-neutral — the business will move |
| `SPOKEN` | 16 | Moments 1 and 4. Say it, spell it, hear it. No accidental word inside it |
| `APT` | 10 | Does it say something true about the business? Useful, not load-bearing |
| `BUYABLE` | 6 | Moment 7. Can it be registered today |

**The TLD is folded into `LEGIT` rather than scored separately.** Evidence: `.com` is typed
**~6x more** than `.co` when people guess, and scores **44% memorability against 33%** [V] —
but **no published study isolates the extension as a conversion cause**, so it is a modest
signal inside legitimacy, not a line of its own.

## 3. The top five, in full

### 1. **Crucible Talent** — 87  ·  `.com` + `.co`

A crucible tests and refines under heat, and in plain English **is** a severe test. Latinate weight, so it reads incorporated rather than clever. Atlassian's Crucible is a code-review tool and Sony's is a game — neither in the trade class

### 2. **Signet Talent** — 83  ·  `.com` + `.co`

A signet is a seal of authenticity. **Signet Jewelers is NYSE-listed**, which is why the word carries corporate weight rather than boutique. Loses on `SPOKEN` — heard as *cygnet*

### 3. **Ballast Talent** — 83  ·  `.com` + `.co`

Ballast keeps a ship steady in weather. The word already lives in the finance register — Ballast Point, Ballast Rock Capital — so it reads like a firm

### 4. **Almanac Talent** — 82  ·  `.com` + `.co`

A book of tables and records. **No collision found anywhere.** Slightly boutique, and people hesitate spelling it

### 5. **Laurel Talent** — 78  ·  `.com` + `.co`

Laurels are the award for excellence. Reads like a boutique showbiz talent agency, and *resting on one's laurels* is the wrong association

## 4. What adding `LEGIT` did

It reordered the middle of the table, and the reason is worth stating.

| Name | Before | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Top Drawer** | 2nd | 8th | A lovely idiom that reads **consumer boutique**, on a `.co`. Wrong signal for someone about to send money abroad |
| **Sure Hands** · **Handiwork** | 5th · 7th | 12th · 13th | Both read as artisan or home-service brands. Charming, and not what a CFO wires to |
| **Handpicked** | 11th | 16th | The most apt single word found, on the TLD that most says *side project* |
| **Crucible Talent** | 1st | **1st** | Unchanged. It was already the only name scoring well on both meaning and weight |
