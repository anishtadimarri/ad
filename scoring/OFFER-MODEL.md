# Offer Model v3 — built on researched competitor pricing

Generated by [`scoring/offer_model.py`](offer_model.py). Placed salary **$20,000** · CPL **$75** · replacement rate 49% · 36-month lifetime.

## Recruitment cost, itemised

Previously buried in two percentages — 3.5% search labour + 1.5% candidate ads —
with tool licences at a token $50 and the conversion course missing entirely.

| Line | Per placement @ $20,000 salary | Scales with |
|---|---|---|
| sourcing & screening labour | $310 | salary (seniority) |
| candidate acquisition media | $372 | salary (seniority) |
| recruiting tool licences | $229 | volume per seat, not salary |
| work-sample grading (per search) | $141 | searches opened, not fills |
| interview & offer coordination | $149 | barely scales |
| conversion course (2 weeks) | $186 | fixed per trained candidate |
| screening pass-throughs | $217 | fixed — billed on at 1.6x |
| comms / e-sign / storage | $62 | fixed |
| **Total** | **$1,667** | |

**A free replacement costs $1,189**, not the full $1,667 — the bench is
already trained and the tool licences are already paid. At a 74% claim rate that is a
**$880 reserve per placement.**

### The old aggregate was right by accident; the composition was not

| | Old model | Itemised | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recruiter labour @ $20k | $700 | $370 | -330 |
| Recruiter labour @ $34k | $1,190 | $545 | -645 |
| Recruiter labour @ $50k | $1,750 | $745 | -1,005 |

The old 3.5%-of-salary line treated recruiter time as if it scaled fully with
seniority. Sourcing effort does scale; interview and offer coordination barely do; tool
licences and training do not at all. **The old model over-charged higher salaries, so the
salary lever is stronger than previously shown.**

## Funnel optimisation

Somewhere's actual flow. The deposit gates the **search**, not the close — which is why
their fill rate can run high. An unfunded search never opens.

```
LEAD ──► CALL HELD ──► $500 DEPOSIT ──► SHORTLIST ──► HIRE
        (biggest leak)  (search opens)    (3 in 48h)
```

| Stage | Base | Optimised | Lever |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead → call held | 22% | **30%** | Booking widget on the thank-you page, same-day slots, SMS + email reminder sequence, two qualifying questions on the form to trade volume for intent |
| Call → deposit paid | 35% | **45%** | **Show 2–3 real candidate profiles on the call itself.** Turns *trust me* into *look at these three people*. Only possible because we run one role in one country — Somewhere cannot do this across 18 countries and dozens of roles |
| Deposit → hire | 80% | **88%** | Standing bench, present 3 never 1, replace until matched, anchor salary in writing at intake |

| | Lead→placement | CAC | 30-day |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | 6.16% | $1,432 | **2.19:1** |
| Optimised | 11.88% | $783 | **4.01:1** |

**Funnel work is worth 1.8x on the ratio — more than any pricing
change in the table above, and it costs nothing but craft.**

### Which stage to fix first

| Stage | −25% | base | +25% | +50% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lead → call | 1.71:1 | 2.19:1 | 2.64:1 | 3.05:1 |
| Call → deposit | 1.64:1 | 2.19:1 | 2.74:1 | 3.28:1 |
| Deposit → hire | 1.64:1 | 2.19:1 | 2.60:1 | 2.60:1 |

All three are near-equivalent in leverage, so fix them in cost order: the reminder
sequence is free, candidate profiles on the call cost one recruiter-hour, bench depth
costs standing capital.

### The volume slide has a breakeven

Somewhere slides 35% down to 25% on volume. The slide **costs** margin on repeat seats and
only pays if it buys enough extra seats to cover the discount.

| | Repeat seats | Lifetime GP | Lifetime ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| S5  S4 + 20% two-seat | 0.80 | $7,564 | **9.66:1** |
| S6  S5 + slide to 27%, no volume gained | 0.80 | $7,097 | **9.07:1** |
| S6b S5 + slide to 27% that WORKS | 1.10 | $7,945 | **10.15:1** |

Discounting 35% → 27% gives up **23%** of the fee on every repeat seat, so the slide
must lift repeat seats by more than 23% — from 0.80 to **0.98+** — to break even. **Do not publish a slide until repeat
behaviour is measured.** Offer it deal-by-deal on request instead.

## The full model landscape

| Model | Market pricing | Wins when |
|---|---|---|
| Contingency placement | 15–30% of salary; offshore specialists **25–35%** | **Under 15–20 hires/yr** |
| Zero-fee flat hourly | Virtustant **$7–8/hr all-in**, median $8.00 across 2,018 placements | Client refuses a fee, accepts markup |
| Managed monthly seat | **$1,250–2,600/mo** bookkeeper; spread ~$800–1,000/mo | Client wants zero employment burden |
| Monthly fee per hire | Pearl Talent — monthly per hire by role complexity | Blend of both |
| Per-FTE / hourly | 1840 & Co **under $25/hr**, min engagement **~$10,000** | Larger, custom engagements |
| Subscription recruiting | **$2,000–10,000/mo** (refined $3,000–8,000). Cuts cost **50–70%** vs contingency for multi-hire | **Senior salaries, multiple hires** |
| RPO | **$3,000–10,000/hire** or **$8,000–15,000/mo** per embedded recruiter | **15–25+ roles/yr** |
| Retained search | **21–31%**, paid in thirds | Executive |

### Why the percentage model is structurally right at $22k

Subscription and RPO win on **high salaries and high volume**, because a flat fee does not
grow with salary while a percentage does. Inverted at the mass-market salary band:

| Client hires/yr | Our 35% of $22k | Subscription at $3,000/mo | Winner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $7,000 | $36,000 | **us** |
| 2 | $14,000 | $36,000 | **us** |
| 3 | $21,000 | $36,000 | **us** |
| 5 | $35,000 | $36,000 | **us** |
| 8 | $56,000 | $36,000 | subscription |
| 12 | $84,000 | $36,000 | subscription |

**Below ~5 hires a year we are structurally cheaper than any subscription competitor, and
cannot be undercut.** Above that we lose the account to a flat fee. So the volume slide is
not a nice-to-have — it is the defence on multi-hire accounts. Keep it unpublished and bring
it out at hire five.

## The guarantee is the cheapest differentiation available

Every player offers the same **6-month replacement**. It is a commodity. But a
*stronger-sounding* guarantee can cost **less** if you change what is being guaranteed —
because labour is a cheaper currency than cash.

| | Claim rate | Refunds | Reserve/placement | 30-day GP | 30-day |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20% two-seat | 49% | cash on 15% of claims | $1,024 | $4,089 | **5.22:1** |
| 12mo UNLIMITED replace, no refunds | 74% | **none — labour only** | $880 | $4,262 | **5.44:1** |

**Doubling the guarantee window to 12 months AND making replacements unlimited is
*cheaper* than the 6-month industry standard**, because eliminating cash refunds saves more
than the extra claims cost. Best guarantee in the market, at negative cost.

## The EOR line — Somewhere's fifth product

Somewhere sells **five** products, not two: Direct Hire · Talent On-Demand · **Somewhere EOR** · Somewhere Equipment · Somewhere Browser. EOR pricing undisclosed.

| Provider | India EOR price / employee / month |
|---|---|
| Wisemonk (India-focused) | **$99–200** |
| Tarmack (India) | from **$199** |
| Multiplier (global) | from **$400**, $250–300 at 50+ |
| Deel (global) | **$599**, $400–500 at 20+ |
| Remote (global) | **$599** |
| Oyster (global) | **$699** |
| Market overall | $199–1,200; most pay **$400–700**; median **~$399** |

**The arbitrage inside the arbitrage:** global platforms charge $499–699 for the same
India employment that India-domiciled providers deliver at $99–200. They are amortising
100+ country entities and enterprise sales. **You have the local cost base and can price
against the global comparison.** Modelled at **$399/mo** with **$55** marginal cost
= **86% gross margin**, plus the industry-standard 2% FX spread.

### Why EOR beats a salary markup as the recurring layer

| | Salary markup (managed seat) | **EOR flat fee** |
|---|---|---|
| Compatible with salary transparency | ❌ breaks it — the spread is hidden | ✅ separate fee for a distinct service |
| Client's alternative | hire direct and cut you out | **Deel at $599, or illegal** |
| Worker outcome | neutral | ✅ **gets PF, ESI, gratuity, benefits** |
| Retention mechanism | none | ✅ **PF compounds, gratuity vests at 5 years — enforced by the state** |
| Gross margin | ~81% | **~86%** |

In the handoff model the client employs the worker as a contractor — which carries
misclassification risk and leaves the worker with no statutory benefits. EOR fixes both.
**It is a better retention mechanism than the stay bonus**, because provident fund and
gratuity are golden handcuffs the government enforces rather than ones we fund.

### Two ways to deliver it — and the first one is right to start

| | White-label a partner | Own entity |
|---|---|---|
| Wholesale cost | **$99–199/employee/mo** (Wisemonk, Tarmack) [V] | **~$55/mo** marginal [E] |
| Resell at $399 → margin | **50–75%** | **~86%** |
| Employment liability | ✅ **none — the partner is the employer** | ❌ you are the legal employer |
| Gratuity / termination exposure | ✅ partner's | ❌ yours, accruing |
| Payroll float | ✅ partner's problem | ❌ ~$1,833/employee/month if invoiced in arrears |
| Time to launch | ✅ **days** | ❌ entity, registrations, counsel |
| Contract Labour Act / secondment risk | ✅ partner's compliance | ❌ needs real Indian employment counsel |

**Start white-label.** You give up ~25 points of margin and you avoid becoming an employer
in a jurisdiction with gratuity accrual and termination law — which is the exact liability the
handoff model was chosen to escape (MASTER §6.1). Bring it in-house once the line is proven
and volume justifies counsel. The float is solved either way by the market-standard 1–2 month
security deposit collected from the **client**, which is negative working capital.
